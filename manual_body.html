1. The Strategy

The Goal: find the handful of NASDAQ-100 stocks that are genuinely leading the market, buy them only when the market backdrop supports them, cut the ones that fail, and let the ones that work run for about three months. All of this stays a small add-on to a core of index funds that do the heavy lifting.

Why I'm Doing This

I've built a system that does what most investors never manage: it watches the whole NASDAQ-100 for me every single day, tells me exactly when the odds are on my side, and keeps me out when they aren't. When it's working the way the backtest says it can, each trade beats QQQ by about 4½ points in three months and wins more than half the time, and the best ones run far beyond that while the stop keeps the misses small. Stack a few of those cycles a year and the satellite can outrun the index by 5+ points a year, while the core compounds underneath it. Keep feeding it $200 a month and that's about $42,000 in 10 years and $87,000 in 15 at QQQ-like returns; lift the whole portfolio just two points and 15 years becomes over $100,000. Along the way I'm building the skill that separates winners from everyone else: following the rules when it's hard, cutting losers fast, and letting winners run. As the account grows, the same system opens the door to bigger positions and selling puts. Every boring "no new buys" day is part of the plan, and every disciplined trade is a step toward it.

The Idea Behind It. Strong stocks tend to stay strong for a while. Stocks that have beaten the market over the past year, and are still beating it, have historically kept outperforming over the next several months. This is momentum, one of the most consistently documented patterns in market research. It happens partly because big investors build positions over weeks rather than days, and partly because good news gets priced in gradually. The system tries to catch that persistence early and safely:

  • Relative strength (each stock vs QQQ, charted with Point & Figure) asks: is it beating the market, not just rising with it?
  • Momentum and trend ask: has it been strong for a year, and is it still in a healthy uptrend?
  • The market regime asks: is the overall tide rising? Even leaders struggle when QQQ is below its trend. This turned out to be the single most important rule.
  • Wyckoff volume patterns ask: are there signs that buyers have been quietly absorbing supply? This one refines the ranking; it isn't required.
  • The red-flag check asks: is there anything the chart can't see, such as falling earnings estimates or a takeover rumor?

The Process: A Funnel.

QQQ in an uptrend? If not, stop here: no new stock buys.
2

The NASDAQ-100 (downloaded fresh from Nasdaq.com each run), plus anything on your watchlist. If that download fails, the run falls back to a built-in list of 30 mega-caps and the report shows a yellow warning, so treat that day's results with caution.
3

Uptrend, top-half momentum, outperforming QQQ, not overextended, no earnings within 7 days, not too volatile, liquid, not already at its P&F target. Usually 80–95% of stocks fail here.

5

Skip anything with estimate cuts or deal-driven news; read the rest.
6

Buy at the next open, stop at 5×ATR below, risk about 1% of the account, hold about 60 trading days.
7

Every signal is logged. Judge the system once, after 6–12 months, against QQQ.

Every Test, and Why It's There:

Tags show how much evidence stands behind each one: Backtested tested in this system's 5-year backtest · Research-backed supported by published research or long practice · Judgment a sensible rule that hasn't been tested on its own. Gates were tested together as a set, not one by one.

Market
Market RegimeBacktested Research-backed
Checks: QQQ above its 200-day average, and its 50-day above its 200-day.
Why: Breakouts fail far more often in falling markets. In the backtest, setups taken in CAUTION markets returned about 0% and trailed QQQ by ~4%: the strongest finding of all.
Trend and Momentum
TrendResearch-backed
Checks: Close above the 50-day, 50-day above a rising 200-day.
Why: Only buy stocks already in an established uptrend; never try to catch a falling one.
12-1 Month MomentumResearch-backed
Checks: The return from 12 months ago to 1 month ago, ranked within the NASDAQ-100; must be top half. Worth 30 score points.
Why: Momentum is one of the most consistently documented return patterns in finance (Jegadeesh & Titman, 1993, and decades since). Skipping the latest month avoids short-term reversals.
Near the 52-Week HighResearch-backed
Checks: Within 25% of the high to pass (part of the trend gate); 10 score points if within 10% of the high, 5 if within 15%.
Why: Stocks near their highs have historically kept outperforming, partly because investors are slow to bid above old highs (the "52-week-high effect").
Relative Strength and Point & Figure
Relative Strength vs QQQResearch-backed
Checks: The stock's price divided by QQQ's, every day.
Why: Separates true leaders from stocks simply riding a rising market. A stock can rise and still be losing to the index.
Point & Figure ChartingResearch-backed
Checks: Plots only meaningful moves as columns of X's (rising) and O's (falling), ignoring time and small wiggles. A BUY signal is a column that rises above the previous high column (a double-top breakout).
Why: It filters out noise and gives objective, rule-based signals with no interpretation needed. It's the method behind the Dorsey Wright relative-strength system.
RS P&F Buy Signal (Gate)Research-backed
Checks: The relative-strength line must be on a P&F BUY. Score points for a recent signal (within 20 days) and a rising column.
Why: The core leadership test: the stock has broken out relative to the market, not just in price.
Price P&F Buy (Score)Judgment
Checks: The stock's own price chart is on a P&F BUY. Worth 10 points.
Why: Confirms that the absolute price is breaking out too, not only the ratio.
Volatility-Scaled BoxesBacktested
Checks: Each stock's P&F box size is set from its own volatility (ATR), not a fixed 1%.
Why: Makes a signal mean the same thing on a calm stock and a wild one. Tested against fixed boxes: a tie, kept for consistency.
Entry Timing and Risk Filters
Not ExtendedJudgment
Checks: No more than 5 ATRs above the 50-day average.
Why: Avoids chasing a stock after a vertical run, when a pullback is likely and the stop would be far away.
No Earnings Within 7 DaysJudgment
Checks: Blocks a buy right before a scheduled report.
Why: An earnings reaction is close to a coin flip and can gap through any stop.
Volatility LimitJudgment
Checks: Average daily range (ATR) no more than 6% of price.
Why: Wildly volatile stocks need stops so wide that positions become tiny and losses deep. Added after MRNA scored 100 on a news spike with a 22% stop.
LiquidityJudgment
Checks: At least $25M traded per day on average.
Why: Ensures you can get in and out at fair prices. Every NASDAQ-100 stock passes; it matters for watchlist names.
Room to TargetBacktested
Checks: The price hasn't already reached its P&F measured-move target.
Why: BUY signals that had already met their target lagged in both 2021–23 (−3.1% vs QQQ, losing money) and 2024–26 (+4.0% vs +6.0% for the rest). Blocking them improved every measure for new entries: margin over QQQ from +3.9% to +4.6%, the median from −0.6% to −0.1%, the share beating QQQ from 48% to 50%, the trade return from +5.1% to +5.8%, and the trade win rate from 51% to 54%, with about a quarter fewer trades.
Wyckoff Volume Patterns (Score Only)
Selling Climax → Secondary TestJudgment
Checks: A high-volume down day (2× normal), a rally of 5%+, then a retest of the low on much lighter volume. Worth 10 points.
Why: Classic sign that heavy selling is exhausted and buyers are absorbing supply.
Sign of StrengthJudgment
Checks: After the test: a wide-range up day closing near its high on 1.5× normal volume. 15 points, plus 5 if it closes above the earlier rally high.
Why: Shows demand taking control. Earnings reactions and 4%+ gap-ups never count, because news moves aren't accumulation.
What the Backtest SaidBacktested
Checks: On its own, a Wyckoff Sign of Strength did about as well as a random stock, and scores of 75+ (mostly from Wyckoff points) did no better than 60–75.
Why: So Wyckoff refines the ranking but carries little of the edge. It's kept with its original weight because a reweighting fitted to the data did worse out of sample.
The Threshold
Score of 60 or MoreBacktested
Checks: The 0–100 score must reach 60, on top of passing every gate.
Why: Stocks that passed the gates but scored under 60 did no better than the baseline; 60+ carried the edge.
Red-Flag Check (Veto Only)
Earnings Estimate RevisionsResearch-backed
Checks: Analysts raising vs cutting this year's EPS forecasts over 30 days. More cuts = VETO?.
Why: Estimate revisions are a well-documented signal (the basis of the Zacks Rank). Rising prices with falling estimates is a warning.
Analyst DowngradesJudgment
Checks: Upgrades vs downgrades over 30 days. Any downgrade = REVIEW.
Why: Prompts a closer look; not decisive on its own.
Negative and Deal HeadlinesJudgment
Checks: Recent headlines scanned for words like lawsuit, probe, guidance cut; takeover or merger news = VETO?.
Why: Catches what price data can't see. A deal-driven price jump isn't the trend the system is buying.
Trade Management
Stop at 5×ATRBacktested
Checks: Exit if the price falls 5 average daily ranges below entry.
Why: Tested at 2.5, 3.5 and 5× and no stop: tight stops were hit on normal pullbacks; 5× earned more with a worst trade near −35%. Also tested against trailing styles (ATR trail, breakeven, P&F trail): the fixed stop earned the most, and trailing never reduced the worst loss. Set it once and leave it.
1% Risk SizingResearch-backed
Checks: Size so that hitting the stop loses about 1% of the account.
Why: A standard risk-control rule: no single trade can do serious damage, so a losing streak is survivable.
60-Trading-Day HoldBacktested
Checks: Hold about three months unless stopped out.
Why: The horizon the backtest measured, and roughly how long momentum tends to persist.
Measured-Move TargetJudgment
Checks: Height of the breakout column projected from the breakout level.
Why: A rough reward-vs-risk check, display only. The classic 3× count gave unrealistic +100–200% targets.

The Principles That Hold It Together:

  • Rules over opinions. The same tested rules every day, applied automatically. Your judgment goes into following them, not into overriding them.
  • Risk before reward. The stop and the position size are set before you buy. Losses stay small; a few large winners carry the results.
  • Outside information can only say no. News, analysts and newsletters can veto a trade, never create one.
  • Evidence before changes. Every rule here earned its place in a 5-year backtest that included the 2022 bear market. Changes get tested the same way, and settings aren't tuned to make the past look better.
  • Doing nothing is a valid result. Most days the answer is "no new buys", and that's the system working.
  • The core comes first. Monthly index-fund contributions are the main engine. The screener is a disciplined way to try for a few extra points on a small slice.

What It Is Not: a forecast, a day-trading system, or a way to grow a small account fast. It doesn't know what the market will do tomorrow. It only tilts the odds toward stocks that are already behaving like leaders, and keeps you out when conditions are poor.

2. The Plan in One Minute

  • Core (most of your money): automatic monthly buys of a broad index fund (VOO/VTI and/or QQQM). No screening, no timing. Keep buying through CAUTION markets.
  • Satellite (a small slice): individual NASDAQ-100 stocks the screener flags as BUY, only when the stock regime is RISK-ON, every gate passes, the score is 60+, and the red-flag check doesn't veto it.
  • Optional high-risk slice: a small fixed amount in bitcoin (e.g. IBIT) if you want it. Treat it as money you can lose.
  • Where it lives: screener trades in the Roth, index funds in a regular account (section 3).
  • The screener never trades. It finds and checks candidates. You decide and place orders.

Honest expectation: the edge is real but modest (a few points per trade over QQQ in backtests). Contributions matter far more than stock picking at small account sizes.

3. Account Strategy

The Decision: run the screener trades inside a Roth, where every winning trade is tax-free for good. Trades are held about 60 days, so in a regular account every gain would be taxed as ordinary income. The trade-off, accepted on purpose: losses in a Roth can't be deducted. A deduction only returns your tax rate (roughly a quarter of a loss) and only in a net losing year, while the Roth keeps 100% of the gains in every winning year. For a system expected to make money, the Roth wins.

Where Each Piece Goes:

Roth IRA
Holds: The screener trades (satellite), cash-secured puts, and core index funds with any room left over
Why: Short-term trading gains are tax-free. Yearly limit set by the IRS ($7,500 for 2026).
Solo 401(k), Roth option
Holds: More room for the system once it has proven itself
Why: Much higher limits for a self-employed owner ($24,500 as the employee for 2026, plus employer contributions from the business). Set up with the CPA.
Regular (taxable) brokerage
Holds: Monthly VOO / QQQM buys above the Roth limits, held long term
Why: Buy-and-hold rarely sells, so it creates little tax, and gains held over a year get the lower long-term rate.
Optional bitcoin slice
Holds: A small fixed amount, e.g. IBIT
Why: Inside the Roth if there's room, so a big run-up is tax-free.

How the Monthly Money Flows:

  1. Fill the Roth first each year (about $625 a month reaches the 2026 limit). Tax-free space is most valuable for the highest-turnover, highest-return money.
  2. Inside the Roth, keep a satellite slice for screener trades. When there's no BUY signal or the regime is CAUTION, that cash waits in a money-market fund, ready for the next signal. The rest of the Roth sits in index funds.
  3. Anything beyond the Roth goes on autopay into VOO or QQQM in the regular account, or into the Solo 401(k) once it's set up.

Roth Rules to Remember:

  • 5-year clock: earnings come out tax-free after age 59½ once the first Roth has been open 5 tax years. The clock starts January 1 of the year of the first contribution, and a contribution for a tax year can be made until that year's April filing deadline (a 2026 contribution made by April 2027 starts the clock at January 1, 2026). Contributions themselves can be withdrawn any time.
  • No required withdrawals from a Roth IRA, ever. It can keep compounding.
  • Earned income needed: contributions can't exceed earned income for the year (business income counts). Direct Roth IRA contributions phase out at $153k–$168k of income (single) or $242k–$252k (married filing jointly) for 2026.
  • No margin: puts must be fully cash-secured, which is how this system uses them anyway. Ask the broker to enable cash-secured puts in the account.
  • Wash-sale trap: never sell a stock at a loss in the regular account and buy it in the Roth within 30 days. That loss is then lost for good.
  • Settlement: Roth IRAs are cash accounts; sale proceeds take a day to settle before reuse. Irrelevant for 60-day holds.
  • Lost money is lost room: a Roth can only be refilled up to the yearly limit, which is why the stop and the 1% sizing matter even more here.

Action Items:

  1. Open a Roth IRA if there isn't one, and make a 2026 contribution (deadline April 2027) to start the 5-year clock.
  2. Ask the CPA about a Solo 401(k) with a Roth option, including the setup deadline to use it for the current year.
  3. Have the broker enable cash-secured puts in the Roth.
  4. Set up the monthly autopay: Roth first, then the regular account.

Tax rules and limits change yearly; confirm with the CPA each year.

5. Your Routine

What It Does: Every Weekday, Automatically (about 4:40 PM Eastern; GitHub sometimes starts 10–30 min late):

  1. Downloads the NASDAQ-100 roster from Nasdaq.com and three years of prices from Yahoo (retries if throttled).
  2. Runs the stock screen vs QQQ, the red-flag check on BUY + top watch names, your watchlist, and the fund screen vs SPY.
  3. Publishes the report, saves a dated copy, logs any BUY to signals.csv.
  4. Sends a notification with a "Do:" line and Open report / Cheat sheet buttons.

What You Do:

  1. Read the "Do:" line. On most days it says no new buys: you're done.
  2. On a BUY day, open the report. For each candidate check the red-flag badge: CLEAN = OK, REVIEW = read the headlines, VETO? = skip.
  3. For a trade you take: buy at the next open, set the stop from the Stop column, size it small (section 8), and note it in your log.
  4. Hold about 60 trading days unless the stop is hit.
  5. Once, after 6–12 months: compare your logged trades with QQQ over the same periods.

6. Reading the Report

  • Status cards: Stock regime (QQQ) decides whether stock buys are allowed. Fund regime (SPY) is context. Buy candidates is the count. Stocks tracked is the total being screened: the NASDAQ-100 plus any watchlist tickers outside it. Tap the number to open All Scores. The small line under it is a data health check (how many NASDAQ-100 stocks downloaded cleanly): gray is normal (90%+), amber means partial data (75–90%, some candidates may be missing), red means a data problem (under 75%): re-run before acting on that day's lists.
  • Yellow note boxes: warnings about the run (roster fallback, partial download, earnings dates missing). Treat that day's results with caution.
  • Buy candidates: passed every gate with score ≥ 60 in a RISK-ON market. Includes the trade plan.
  • Watch list: stocks that are close to a BUY but not there yet. A stock lands here for one of two reasons:
    • It passes every gate, but scores 40–59. It's a qualified leader without enough confirmation yet.
    • Your watchlist: each stock from watchlist.txt with tier, red-flag badge, stop and a plain-English verdict of why it is or isn't a buy.
    • Red-flag check: details for checked names: estimate revisions, analyst actions and flagged headlines (tap to read).
    • It scores 60+, but misses exactly one gate. Most often that's the market regime (CAUTION), or being too stretched above its 50-day average.
    The "What it needs to become a BUY" column spells it out in plain English, such as "Ready except for the market: needs the market regime to turn RISK-ON" or "Needs 8 more score points (now 52), e.g. from a Wyckoff sign of strength (+15)". Watch-list stocks are not trades; they're the likeliest next candidates. Do nothing until one becomes a BUY.
  • Funds (vs SPY): which part of the market is leading. Context, not trades. Shows the top 8 by score plus your funds (★); tap Show all for the full list. Funds are also on the All Scores page (Funds filter) with P&F charts vs SPY.
  • P&F charts: tap any ticker (in the tables, cards or All Scores) to open its Point & Figure charts: the stock's price chart with Now, Stop and Target lines, and its relative-strength chart vs QQQ. Bold columns are the ones that triggered a BUY or SELL signal. Named P&F patterns (double and triple tops, catapults, poles and others) are labeled with short codes and listed in a legend, for learning to recognize them only. They never change a score or decision; decisions come only from the report. A "How to read it" guide is at the bottom of the chart page. Charts reflect the latest daily run.
  • All Scores (link at the top): every stock tracked that day, with its score, tier, red-flag badge and a plain-English reason. Search a ticker or filter by BUY, WATCH, My watchlist or Not a buy. It's the place to check why a stock you expected isn't on the lists. A CSV of the same data is linked on that page.
  • History: past reports and the signal log.

8. The Strategy Rules

Gates (All Must Pass):

GateRule
RegimeQQQ above its 200-day average and its 50-day above its 200-day
TrendClose > 50-day > 200-day, 200-day rising, within 25% of the 52-week high
Momentum12-1 month momentum in the top half of the NASDAQ-100
RS_PnFRelative-strength P&F chart vs QQQ on a BUY signal
NotExtendedNo more than 5 ATRs above the 50-day average
NoEarningsNo earnings report in the next 7 calendar days
VolatilityATR(20) no more than 6% of price
Liquidity50-day average dollar volume of $25M or more
RoomToTargetThe price hasn't already reached its P&F target

Score (0–100): momentum rank 30 · RS P&F quality 20 (buy signal 10, fresh within 20 days 5, rising column 5) · price P&F buy 10 · Wyckoff 30 (ST 10, SOS 15, SOS above rally high 5) · near the 52-week high 10.

BUY = all gates + score ≥ 60. WATCH = gates pass with score 40–59, or score 60+ with exactly one gate failing.

Trade Plan: enter at the next open · stop at close − 5×ATR(20), never moved down · hold ~60 trading days · size so a stop-out loses about 1% of the account: shares = (account × 1%) ÷ (entry − stop).

Selling Puts Instead: only with cash for 100 shares at the strike. A strike near the Stop column fits the system.

P&F charts use closing prices, 3-box reversal, and box sizes scaled to each stock's volatility (1×ATR for price, 1.5× the RS line's average daily move for RS), rounded to 0.25% and kept between 0.5% and 6%.

9. Red-Flag Check

Looks for reasons not to buy. It can veto or flag a stock, never add one.

  • Headlines (last 14 days): Yahoo Finance news scanned for negative terms (downgrade, cuts guidance, misses, lawsuit, probe, SEC, recall, layoffs, CEO exits, short-seller, tariffs...) and deal terms (takeover, merger, acquisition, activist).
  • Analyst actions (30 days): upgrades vs downgrades.
  • Earnings estimates (30 days): whether analysts are raising or cutting this year's EPS forecasts (the signal behind the Zacks Rank).
VerdictMeaning / action
VETO?Estimates being cut, or deal news is driving the price. Skip or wait.
REVIEWNegative headlines or downgrades. Read them; many will be about other companies or harmless.
CLEANNothing found. Proceed per the rules.
NO DATAYahoo returned nothing. Check the news yourself.

Limits: only Yahoo's 10–20 most recent headlines per stock; Yahoo's feed includes related market stories, so keyword hits produce false alarms. That's why headline hits get REVIEW, not VETO.

11. Your Positions

Your holdings live in a private Google Sheet, Screener Positions. After every daily run the system checks each one and writes a recommended action back into the sheet. The action also goes into your notification. Positions never appear on the public report site.

Tabs: Positions is your real account. Paper is pretend money, checked exactly the same way, so you can practise or test the rules. Cash logs money in and out of each account. Compare puts the accounts side by side and against QQQ. To add another account, duplicate the Paper tab and rename it; any tab with "paper" or "test" in its name never puts SELL in the notification title.

You Fill In (yellow headers): Ticker, Account, Entry date, Entry price, Shares, Stop (from the report on the day you bought; it never moves), Notes. Add a row when you buy. When you sell, don't delete the row: fill in Exit date and Exit price. The row is then marked CLOSED and kept as your track record.

The System Fills In (gray headers): Last close, Days held, Health, Action, Why, Updated, and QQQ same period (what QQQ did from your entry to your exit, or to today). Formulas add Gain %, Value, $ at risk to stop, Trade return and vs QQQ (the trade's return minus QQQ's).

Cash Tab: one line per money movement: Date, Account (the tab's name), Type (Deposit, Withdrawal or Interest) and Amount. Your first line for each account is the Deposit you funded it with; add each monthly deposit and the interest from your statement. The system fills in what the same money would be worth if each deposit had bought QQQ that day.

Compare Tab: for each account, money put in, cash on hand (deposits plus interest, minus buys, plus sells), open value, account value, gain and return, then the key test: Ahead of QQQ, account value minus what the same deposits would be worth in QQQ. Below that: closed trades, win rate, average trade return and average vs QQQ per trade.

Health: Strong = it would still qualify as a BUY (ignoring the market regime) · Fading = still in trend but scoring under 60 · Broken = the uptrend broke or its relative-strength chart vs QQQ flipped to SELL.

One Action per Position, checked in this order:

CheckAction
Stop hitSELL at the next open (if your stop order already filled, enter the Exit date and Exit price)
Health brokenSELL: leadership has faded
Earnings within 7 daysHOLD THROUGH EARNINGS if your gain is at least your stop distance; otherwise SELL BEFORE EARNINGS
Red flag VETO?HOLD (stop unchanged) if health is strong; SELL if fading
60-day reviewEXTEND 60 DAYS if strong; SELL if fading
Target metHOLD, stop unchanged: winners carry the results
Fresh BUY againADD (OPTIONAL), only within the 1% risk budget
Everything elseNo action, with a status line (day count, gain, distance to stop)

The notification has one line per account, e.g. "Positions: 2 OK" and "Paper: SELL NVDA". A row missing its entry date, price or stop, or with only half an exit, shows FIX ROW. Any SELL in a real account puts the ticker at the front of the notification title at top priority. Closed rows show CLOSED with the trade's result vs QQQ.

The stop and 60-day hold are backtested; the earnings, red-flag and 60-day extension rules are judgment calls, not yet tested on their own.

14. What the Backtest Showed

Weekly evaluations, Aug 2021 – Aug 2026, current NASDAQ-100 members, 60-day holds, before costs. Survivorship bias flatters everything, so compare against the baseline, not zero.

60 days after signalBaselineBUY new entries, before target gateBUY new entries, current rules
Excess vs QQQ, average+1.6%+3.9%+4.6%
Excess vs QQQ, median−0.6%−0.6%−0.1%
Beat QQQ48%48%50%
Trade return, 5×ATR stop+4.3%+5.1%+5.8%
Trade win rate, 5×ATR stop53%51%54%
Worst trade, 5×ATR stop−51%−35%−35%
  • Regime matters most: setups in CAUTION markets returned about 0% and trailed QQQ by ~4%.
  • The score cutoff matters: stocks passing gates with score < 60 did no better than the baseline. 60–75 did better than 75+.
  • Wider stops did better: 2.5×ATR was hit on normal pullbacks; 5× earned more with a deeper worst case.
  • Skip BUYs that already hit their target: they lagged in both halves of the test (2021–23 and 2024–26). Blocking them improved every measure for new entries: margin over QQQ from +3.9% to +4.6%, the median from −0.6% to −0.1%, the share beating QQQ from 48% to 50%, the trade return from +5.1% to +5.8%, and the trade win rate from 51% to 54%, with about a quarter fewer trades. Bigger upside, though, did not mean better results, so upside isn't used for ranking.
  • P&F charts alone have little edge here; the other filters do the work: over 2021–26, buying NASDAQ-100 stocks on P&F buy signals alone beat QQQ by about the same as picking at random (+1–2 points over 60 days; RS P&F alone did slightly worse than random). Adding the RISK-ON regime raised absolute returns but not the margin over QQQ (+1.5). The full system beat QQQ by +4.1, about 2.5 points more than the best simple P&F rule, with the smallest worst trade (−35%). The momentum, trend and score filters are what pick the leaders.
  • A fixed stop beat every trailing stop: on the same BUY signals, fixed earned +6.3% per trade (new entries), breakeven +6.2%, P&F trail +5.6%, 5×ATR trail +5.0%. No trailing style reduced the worst loss (−35% for all), and the ATR trail got stopped out twice as often (64% vs 30%): the choppy-market problem.
  • A few winners carry the results: the median trade barely beats QQQ.
  • Funds: ranking ETFs vs SPY added only 1–2 points, and stops didn't help.

15. Decisions We Made (and Why)

  • Rewrote the original Gemini code: its "target hit" signal was fake, breakouts weren't real double tops, Wyckoff events weren't required to happen in order, and errors were silently hidden.
  • Added momentum, trend and regime gates with long research records; Wyckoff became a scoring bonus, not a requirement.
  • Kept the original score weights: a reweighting fitted to 2023–26 did worse on 2021–26.
  • ATR-scaled boxes: tied with fixed boxes in testing; kept for consistency across stocks.
  • 1× measured-move target: the classic 3× vertical count produced unrealistic +100–200% targets.
  • Earnings and gap days don't count as a Sign of Strength: news moves aren't accumulation.
  • Left the Dow off: heavy overlap with the NASDAQ-100 and slower stocks where momentum works less well. The S&P 500 would be the better expansion if ever needed.
  • No paid newsletters: unproven, self-reported records, and the fee is a huge hurdle on a small account. Outside research is a veto, never a reason to buy.
  • Room-to-target gate added; upside ranking rejected: "target already met" was the one upside finding that held in both periods, and blocking those BUYs improved every result measure (see section 14). Ranking by % upside mostly favored volatile stocks and would add noise, so the Upside % column was removed.
  • Keep the full system, not a simpler P&F-only version: tested head to head, the extra gates and scoring added about 2.5 points per trade over QQQ compared with the best simple P&F rule.
  • Keep the stop fixed: raising stops as a stock climbs (ATR trail, breakeven, P&F trail) was tested and lost to leaving the stop alone. Set it at entry and don't move it, up or down.
  • Screener trades in a Roth: 60-day holds would be taxed as income in a regular account. Giving up the loss deduction is worth it, because the deduction only returns about a quarter of a net loss while the Roth keeps all of every gain (section 3).
  • Crypto: only BTC/ETH have a reasonable case, as a small slice. Altcoins fail the volatility gate and are mostly gambling.
