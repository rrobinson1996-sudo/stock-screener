How to read it
  • X = price rising one box; O = falling one box. A new column starts only after a 3-box reversal, so small wiggles are ignored. Time isn't on the axis.
  • Bold column = that column triggered a signal: an X column above the previous X high is a BUY (double-top breakout); an O column below the previous O low is a SELL.
  • Numbers along the bottom = month each column started (1–9, A=Oct, B=Nov, C=Dec), with the year marked where it changes.
  • Lines: Now = latest close · Stop = 5×ATR stop · Target = measured-move objective.
  • Relative-strength chart: the stock divided by QQQ. Rising X's mean it's beating the index, the core leadership test.
  • Why the two charts use different box sizes: each box is sized to how much that line normally moves, so a signal means the same thing on a calm stock and a volatile one. The price box is 1× the stock's average daily range (ATR over 20 days) as a percent of price. The relative-strength line has no daily high or low, so its box is 1.5× its average daily move over 100 days. It usually moves less than the price, so its boxes are often smaller. Both are rounded to 0.25% and kept between 0.5% and 6%, and they're set fresh each day from the latest data.
  • The chart opens at the latest column; swipe left to see history.
Chart Patterns (for learning only)

Named P&F patterns are marked on the charts with short codes so you can learn to recognize them. They don't change any score or decision; buy and sell decisions come only from the report.

CodePatternWhat it shows
DTDouble top breakoutAn X column rises above the previous X column. The basic P&F buy signal.
TTTriple top breakoutBreaks above two earlier highs at the same level: more selling was absorbed before it broke through.
STTSpread triple topA triple top whose two earlier highs are farther apart, with a lower high between them.
CATBullish catapultA triple top breakout, a small pullback, then another breakout: the move continuing.
BSRBearish signal reversedA sell signal that immediately failed: the next X column broke back above the prior high.
LPLow poleA sharp drop to a new low, then a rebound of at least half of it: sellers may be exhausted.
DBDouble bottom breakdownAn O column falls below the previous O column. The basic P&F sell signal.
TBTriple bottom breakdownBreaks below two earlier lows at the same level.
STBSpread triple bottomA triple bottom whose two earlier lows are farther apart.
BCATBearish catapultA triple bottom breakdown, a small bounce, then another breakdown.
BUSRBullish signal reversedA buy signal that immediately failed: the next O column broke below the prior low.
HPHigh poleA tall rally to a new high, then a pullback of more than half of it: the rally may be fading.
LTLong tail downA drop of 20 or more boxes in one column, often a washout near a low.

Each chart lists its patterns with dates under "Patterns on this chart". A breakout pattern is dated the day the column broke through; poles and long tails are dated by the column's latest box. Green codes sit above the column (bullish), red below (bearish). The chart shows about the last 60 columns, so a pattern that started earlier may not be labeled.
