Cheat sheet

ReportManual
What to do with the daily report.

1. Check the stock regime first

CAUTION CAUTION (QQQ below its trend): no new stock buys. In the 2021–26 backtest, signals taken in CAUTION markets returned about 0% over 60 days and trailed QQQ by about 4%.

RISK-ON RISK-ON: buy candidates are live.

This only governs the stock screener. Monthly buys of your index funds continue either way.

2. Which candidates to take

  • Score 60 or more and every gate passed = BUY candidate. Below 60 did no better than a random stock.
  • Don't favor 75+ over 60–75. In the backtest, scores of 60–75 beat QQQ by about 4% over 60 days and 75+ by about 2.6%. A high score isn't a stronger buy.
  • Red flags decide, not the score:
    CLEAN take it  ·  REVIEW read the headlines (skip ones about other companies)  ·  VETO? skip or wait — estimates are being cut or deal news is driving the price  ·  NO DATA check the news yourself
  • Never add a stock the screener didn't flag, however good the press is.

3. How to place the trade

  • Entry: at the next market open.
  • Stop: the Stop column (close − 5.0×ATR). Set it when you enter and don't move it down.
  • Size: small. Risk about 1% of the account per trade: shares = (account × 1%) ÷ (entry − stop). Fractional shares are fine.
  • Hold: about 60 trading days (~3 months), then reassess. Exit early only if the stop is hit.
  • Target / Upside: a rough measured-move target, not a forecast. Use it to check reward vs risk: Upside ÷ Risk of 1.5–2 or better is worth taking. "met" = target already reached.
  • Earnings: the screener won't flag a stock within 7 days of its report. Note the Next earnings date; it will usually fall inside your hold.

4. What to expect

  • New BUY signals beat QQQ about 51% of the time, by about +3 to +5 points on average over 60 days (backtest, before costs).
  • The median trade only barely beats QQQ. A few big winners carry the results, so don't sell winners early.
  • Expect losing streaks and a worst trade around −30%. That's normal, not a reason to change the settings.

5. Funds section

Context only: it shows which part of the market is leading. Ranking funds beat SPY by only 1–2 points in the backtest, and stops didn't help funds, so don't trade funds off it.

6. Habits

  • Every BUY signal is logged automatically in signals.csv. Note what you actually did.
  • Don't tweak settings after a bad week. Review once, after 6–12 months, against QQQ.
  • Selling puts: only with cash to cover 100 shares at the strike; a strike near the Stop column fits the system.

7. Phone commands

Type these in the Message box of your command topic in ntfy (not the report topic), or use GitHub → Actions → Watchlist commands → Run workflow.

SendWhat it does
add MANHAdds to your watchlist and replies with the full check
add MANH, COSTSeveral at once (commas or spaces)
remove COSTTakes it off your watchlist
check NVDA FASTReplies with the check; watchlist unchanged
NVDASame as check
listReplies with your current watchlist
helpReplies with this command list

Timing: ntfy commands are picked up every 15 minutes, 6 AM–10 PM Central; replies usually take 15–30 minutes. The GitHub form replies in about 2 minutes. Messages sent overnight are picked up in the morning, but ntfy only keeps messages for about 12 hours.

8. Command cautions

  • remove only edits your watchlist. It never touches the NASDAQ-100 scan, which is downloaded fresh every run. A NASDAQ-100 stock you remove still appears in the main lists.
  • Limits: up to 25 stocks on the watchlist and 10 checks per command run. Each check downloads the whole NASDAQ-100 (about a minute), and a long list slows the daily run and invites Yahoo to throttle it.
  • Don't send the same command over and over. Messages are batched, so five quick messages become one run. Resending won't make the reply come faster.
  • Typos get added as-is. "add APPL" puts APPL on the list and the report will say "no price data". Send remove APPL to fix it.
  • Funds don't belong on the watchlist. ETFs get judged against QQQ there, which isn't meaningful; use the Funds section instead.
  • Same rules apply. A watchlist stock is only a buy if the regime is RISK-ON, every gate passes and the score is 60+. Adding it doesn't lower the bar.
  • Commands never trade. They only change the list and send checks. Buying and selling stay with you.
  • Keep your topic names private. Anyone who knows the command topic could edit your watchlist (nothing else). Every change is saved in watchlist.txt's history on GitHub, so mistakes can be undone.
Numbers come from the 2021–26 backtest on current NASDAQ-100 members (flattered by survivorship, no costs). Research tool, not financial advice.
